# Pego review, 8 to 11 November 2026

Records of the option and ranking review. Nothing here is saved to `pego-product.study.json`, and no engine code changed on this branch. Runs use the engine on `main` at ec731cf (with the permit penalty, decision 245). Each run takes about 40 seconds and needs about 8 GB of memory (`node --max-old-space-size=8192`). The scripts are in `review-2026-11-11/`.

## 1. Re-run at the study's 900 MW IT load

- The saved study's inputs were re-run on the current engine: 9,592 options screened, 5,634 adequate. The study's README says 2,000 options; this is not explained (OPEN-QUESTIONS 246b).
- Ranking with the agreed rule (adequate first, then only "Does not meet" below, then score): the top five strategies, by bridge, are shown below. All meet resilience, all carry HVO N+1 standby.
  1. Rented gas bridge kept as prime, 220 MW, PV 60 MWp: score 84.9, €140.7/MWh, capex about €1,039 m, 223 g/kWh. Its permit is rated "uncertain", which is not penalised (246a).
  2. HVO-diesel bridge kept as prime: rank 127 after the penalty (was 1st, score 85.0 before it, €138.9/MWh, capex €1,149 m). Permits rated unlikely (prime) and unlikely/uncertain (standby).
  3. Gas recips bridge kept as prime: rank about 20 before the penalty, €144.1/MWh.
  4. Gas turbines bridge kept as prime: rank about 48 before the penalty, €141.6/MWh.
  5. Diesel bridge with shortfall kept as prime and the rest as backup: rank about 60 before the penalty, €152.5/MWh.

## 2. Option 1 (HVO-diesel prime) in detail, before the penalty

- Backed load 1,990 MW against 1,350 MW critical load. Known issue 39: the leased Elecgás plant (427.5 MW) counts as firm prime; without it backed load is 1,562 MW, still "Meets".
- Diesel runs about 8,600 hours in year 2 (85% of demand), about 1,000 hours in year 4, and not at all from year 5. It is kept installed as prime power for 20 years.
- Permit outlook: HVO-diesel prime gensets "unlikely" (ready month 24, needed month 24, no float); HVO standby gensets "likely" but three months late.

## 3. Leased-plant option (Elecgás as bridge and firm supply)

- The best version in the run is rank 331: Elecgás lease kept as prime, gas-engine top-up, HVO N+1 standby, 60 MWp PV. Score 79.6, €172.3/MWh, capex €1,979 m, 267 g/kWh.
- Reasons it ranks lower: the lease (855 MW) is short of the design peak (about 1,368 MW), and the gap is filled with gas engines; the lease rate is a judgement default (€6/kW a month); the owner's consent is not modelled.

## 4. Pego at 500 MW IT (not saved)

- Design peak about 760 MW. 7,477 of 9,592 options adequate.
- Top five: rented gas bridge removed at grid arrival, gas-engine top-up, 20 to 33 MWp PV, HVO N+1 standby. Score 88.7 to 89.1, €124.5 to €127.8/MWh, capex €936 m to €1,067 m, 143 to 145 g/kWh. None has an "unlikely" permit. The fifth is "Meets with flags".
- Caveat: the lower IT load changes the project, so these figures are not comparable with the 900 MW study.

## Reproducing

From this folder: `node --max-old-space-size=8192 review-2026-11-11/rerank2.mjs` (set `OUT` to an output path), `IT=500 OUT=... node --max-old-space-size=8192 review-2026-11-11/lowload.mjs`. The scripts read `studies/pego-rev3/pego-product.study.json` and `app/src/engine/`.
