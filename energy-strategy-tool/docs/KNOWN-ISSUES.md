# Known issues in the built program

Found on 2026-10-21 while writing the user guide, by checking the guide against the code on `main` (commit 441635e) and a full run of the T1 example; items 10–13 were found later. **Only item 13 is fixed** (2026-10-23, with the owner's approval). Each changes numbers or wording that the owner has seen, so ask the owner before fixing any of them (OPEN-QUESTIONS 31), one at a time, and record the answer here. Line numbers are for commit 441635e and will drift.

The user guide describes each one for engineers: chapter 54 ("Findings while writing this guide"), with details in chapters 16, 22, 37 and 47 (`user-guide/src/`).

| # | Issue | Where in the code | Effect (T1 example) | Possible fix | Decision needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Repeated year not fully steady.** The last explicitly dispatched year is repeated to the end of the evaluation period, but when the last change falls mid-year that year is still ramping | `app/src/engine/dispatch.js`, `dispatchYears()` (line 115) | The last change is month 66, the middle of 2032, so 2032 still has six months of phase 4 ramping. Repeating it for 2033–2046 undercounts facility energy by about 1.8 % a year (777,471 MWh instead of 791,905). Sizing is not affected (it uses the first full year after the ramp) | Dispatch one more year when the last change is not in month 0 of a year | Yes: changes every option's ledger, LCOE and carbon slightly |
| 2 | **Network charge on PPA energy is not escalated.** Electricity escalation multiplies the grid energy cost (which includes the non-commodity adder) but not the same adder on PPA energy | `app/src/engine/evaluate.js`, `economics()` (line 247: `L.grid += d.gridCost * elecEsc + d.ppaNetworkCost`) | None at the default escalation of 0 %; with escalation above 0 %, PPA options' network cost is understated | Multiply `ppaNetworkCost` by the same escalation | Yes: whether the adder escalates at all, and consistently |
| 3 | **Export income ignores the price multiplier.** Import is priced at `price × multiplier + adder`; export at 90 % of the unscaled wholesale price | `app/src/engine/dispatch.js`, line 337 (`exportRevenue += exp * pPrice[h] * exportFactor`) | None at the default multiplier of 1; the tornado's grid-price driver does not move export income | Apply `priceMult` to the export price | Yes: small, but it changes tornado bars |
| 4 | **Archetypes take the first open value of every choice they do not specify.** With the defaults that makes most archetypes air-cooled with roof heat rejection | `app/src/engine/generator.js`, `archetypeVector()` (line 189) | In T1 most archetypes cannot reach full load and rank low (guide chapter 47) | Archetypes use the engineer's cooling type (decided in the walkthrough) and a stated rule for the other unspecified choices | Yes: the rule for the other choices |
| 5 | **Identical options.** Options that differ only in a choice that changes no number appear as separate rows with identical results | Option generation (`generator.js`) and the n/a collapse rules | T1 ranks 10 and 11 differ only in the heat rejection location of an option with no rooftop solar | Collapse such a choice to "n/a" when it has no effect, like C2 when C1 = wait | Yes; linked to the top-five question (OPEN-QUESTIONS 24). The heat rejection location still matters in the Beta Site tab |
| 6 | **Two density rules disagree.** The IT estimate from area decided in the walkthrough (35 % site coverage, 1 storey, and about 5.5 kW IT per m² of floor from the one benchmark, 450 MW on 82,020 m²; all three to research) and the building massing (whitespace density by cooling × gross-up 2.2, 2 storeys: 0.7–1.8 kW per m² of floor) | Massing: `app/src/engine/defaults.js` (cooling `densityKWm2`, load `grossUp`, `storeys`) and `sizing.js`; the IT estimate is not built yet | For the same site the two differ by a factor of three to eight | Change one during the research of the defaults | Yes: OPEN-QUESTIONS 26 |

| 8 | **"Opex year 1" is the first year of the analysis**, which has no load in T1 (2027), so it shows €0 | `app/src/engine/evaluate.js` line 300 (`opexYear1`); shown in `app/src/components/OptionDetail.jsx` line 90 | Option detail shows "Opex year 1 / steady state: €0 / …" | Use the first year with load, or rename the line | Yes: wording and definition |
| 9 | **"First power" can be month 0**, before any phase exists, because it measures when the supply could energise phase 1 rather than when phase 1 starts | `app/src/engine/evaluate.js`, `timeToPower()` (line 20; the first-power test is at line 37) | All 20 of T1's top options and 10 of its 11 archetypes show first power at month 0 (20 MW of grid is available from the start) | Report the later of the phase 1 date and the supply date, or rename it | Yes: changes time-to-power figures shown |
| 10 | **Gas-dependency flag raised when HVO standby alone covers the critical load.** The resilience check adds retained bridging plant (and battery) to the backed load, then flags "Backup depends on gas network availability during a grid outage" because a gas plant is counted, even when the liquid-fuel gensets alone meet the critical load | `app/src/engine/evaluate.js`, `resilience()` (the `gasDependent` test, near the `res-gas` flag) | Found in the Pego case study (2026-10-22): 261 × 2.75 MW HVO units = 717.8 MW against 715.6 MW critical load, yet the minor flag `res-gas` is shown | Raise the flag only when the critical load cannot be met without the gas-fired plant | Yes: changes a flag on many options |
| 11 | **A plant cannot be held at its minimum stable load by backing off grid import.** When the load a plant must serve is below its minimum stable output and there is no export, dispatch reports unmet load instead of running the plant at minimum and reducing grid import | `app/src/engine/dispatch.js` (firm plant dispatch with `msl`) | Found in the Pego case study (2026-10-22): an existing CCGT with a 45 % minimum load shows 31 GWh of unmet load in hours where grid plus CCGT could serve the campus; the study had to model the minimum load as 0 | Let firm plant run at its minimum and reduce grid import in those hours; report surplus that cannot be absorbed | Yes: changes dispatch, unmet load and the adequacy ranking |
| 12 | **Bridging redundancy always adds a unit, and the library has no "existing plant under lease".** Bridging plant is sized N+1 per block, so a site offering exactly two large units is sized at three; existing plant has to be modelled by re-using the rented-generation record | `app/src/engine/sizing.js` (bridging units `n + ceil(n / blockSize)`); `defaults.js` technology library | Pego case study (2026-10-22): the existing two-unit CCGT was sized at three units for some options; case-specific edits were needed | A unit-count cap or fixed-size option for bridging and prime plant, and an "existing plant" technology type | Yes: new inputs |
| 13 | **Economic plant skipped below its minimum stable load when the grid is full.** A plant cheaper than the grid served "all the remaining demand" as an economic choice; when that remainder was below its minimum stable load it was skipped, even when the grid connection could not take it, leaving the remainder unmet | `app/src/engine/dispatch.js`, firm plant dispatch (the economic branch) | Found 2026-10-23 in work package 5: with a PPA filling the import capacity, 0.2 MW went unmet in about 15 hours (7–8 MWh), so every T1 PPA option was "not adequate" | The plant counts as needed when the remaining demand exceeds the remaining grid capacity, so it runs at least at its minimum load | **Fixed 2026-10-23 with the owner's approval** (branch `feature/ppa-rules`) |
