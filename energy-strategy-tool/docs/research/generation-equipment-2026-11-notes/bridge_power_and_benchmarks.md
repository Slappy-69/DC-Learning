## 1. Rented and bridge power providers: unit sizes, fuel, speed to site, contract length, price

### Takeaway
The global mobile/bridge fleet is anchored by trailer-mounted aeroderivatives of about 30–35 MW (GE TM2500 class, APR Energy fleet), 50 MW packaged aeros (ProEnergy PE6000), and 1.5–25 MW gas-engine blocks (Aggreko, VoltaGrid/INNIO, Caterpillar). Equipment already in a rental fleet can be installed in 2–13 weeks. New-build "mobile" capacity is now sold forward into 2027–2028. In 2026 the biggest US bridge fleets were bought or contracted outright by AI developers (Musk/APR, Oracle/VoltaGrid, Crusoe/ProEnergy), which tightens availability. No provider publishes rental prices per kW-month. In Europe the most visible offer is Aggreko's 1.5 MW gas fleet, grid-code-certified in 11 countries.

**Pricing (closest public proxies; no published rental rate cards found)**
- Behind-the-meter gas generation costs about **US$100–165/MWh (≈€90–148/MWh)**, against US$90–95/MWh for grid-connected alternatives (RaboResearch estimate). Assumptions: 40–65% overbuild, 10-year amortisation, 75–90% capacity factor, Henry Hub gas at $4.00–4.50/MMBtu, recip heat rate 8,000–9,000 Btu/kWh — [Rabobank, "The sprint"](https://www.rabobank.com/knowledge/d011524694-the-sprint-data-centers-are-building-a-parallel-energy-system-in-the-us)
- Illustrative all-in recip-engine power cost **US$125/MWh (≈€112/MWh)** against grid at $66/MWh. The premium equals about 2–6% of disclosed AI compute revenue — [Modo Energy, 10 Sep 2026](https://modoenergy.com/research/us-data-center-onsite-gas-speed-to-power)
- Lazard LCOE+ 2026: gas peaking **US$144–276/MWh**, combined cycle US$51–129/MWh, with gas LCOE at a "15-year high" — [Utility Dive on Lazard 2026](https://utilitydive.com/news/renewables-remain-cheapest-lcoe-rising-lazard/825443/). Lazard 2025 gas peaker range: **US$149–251/MWh**, as cited by [Rabobank](https://www.rabobank.com/knowledge/d011524694-the-sprint-data-centers-are-building-a-parallel-energy-system-in-the-us) [SECONDARY]
- European regulated proxy for temporary generation availability: ESB's **200 MW North Wall temporary emergency generation** contract with EirGrid was **€508.7 m over 5 years** (or €357 m if only 3 years were needed) (Dec 2022) — [BreakingNews.ie](https://www.breakingnews.ie/ireland/emergency-power-plan-for-esb-north-wall-station-to-cost-e508m-over-five-years-1406362.html). Derived: about **€509/kW-yr ≈ €42/kW-month** on the 5-year basis and about **€595/kW-yr ≈ €50/kW-month** on the 3-year basis. These cover availability with ≤500–1,460 run-hours, not energy, and are not a market rental rate [EST]

### Inferences
- Bridge power in practice comes in three tiers. (a) Rental fleet units already built (APR, Aggreko, TM2500 fleets): roughly 2–13 weeks to install if units are free. (b) New-build mobile/modular units (Dynamis, VoltaGrid, PE6000, Boom): about 12 months to more than 2 years from order. (c) Permanent plant. In 2026 the tier (a) fleet is largely absorbed by US AI campuses, so for a European project in 2027–28 the realistic options are probably Aggreko-type 1.5 MW gas blocks plus BESS, or ordering tier (b) equipment.
- Musk's ownership of APR raises the likelihood that APR's >1 GW fleet will prioritise xAI/SpaceX sites. Treat APR availability for third parties in Europe as uncertain.
- The North Wall derived figure of about €40–50/kW-month is a useful order-of-magnitude anchor for "availability-only" temporary capacity in Europe. Fuel (gas at about 8,300–9,500 Btu/kWh heat rate) and O&M come on top for prime-power bridge use.

### Gaps
- **No public rental rates (€/kW-month or €/MWh) were found for any provider** (Aggreko, APR, Cat Rental Power, Cummins, Sunbelt, Herc, Bredenoord, Speedy Hire). Commercial terms are bespoke and confidential.
- Not covered within the research budget: Cummins rental, Sunbelt/Herc power, Bredenoord, Speedy Hire, Stage V rental gas gensets, Solar Turbines mobile units (e.g. SMT130), Siemens SGT-A45 mobile, and Enchanted Rock. For Enchanted Rock I have headline evidence only that it markets "bridge-to-grid" and flexible-connection microgrids — [Latitude Media headline](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/enchanted-rock-is-selling-utilities-on-flexible-data-center-connection/) [HEADLINE ONLY].
- FT8 MobilePac and SGT-A45 unit ratings and rental availability in Europe: not sourced.

## 2. Data centre bridge / on-site power projects 2024–2026 (MW, duration, technology)

### Takeaway
US AI campuses dominate the record. Multi-GW on-site gas programmes from Oracle/VoltaGrid, Crusoe and Nscale/Caterpillar use recips, packaged aeros and mobile turbines, typically as "bridge-to-prime" plants that stay on as backup or flexible capacity. European examples are small, or are system-operator emergency plant (Ireland TEG) rather than developer bridges. Ireland's 2025 connection policy now requires dispatchable on-site or proximate generation for new data centres.

- **Oracle / VoltaGrid (Texas):** 2.3 GW of INNIO Jenbacher-based modular gas across multiple Texas sites (Oct 2025), with Energy Transfer gas supply. Bridge versus permanent was not specified — [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/oracle-taps-voltagrid-for-2-3-gw-modular-gas-fleet-to-power-ai-data-centers-across-texas/); 92 × ~25 MW packs — [DCF](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55325092/innio-and-voltagrid-landmark-23-gw-modular-power-deal-signals-new-phase-for-ai-data-centers)

- **Ireland – system-level temporary emergency generation (TEG), not a developer bridge:** ESB's **North Wall 193 MW** (connected Dec 2023) and **Shannonbridge 224 MW** (available Aug 2024), plus 200 MW from other parties, for up to 650 MW in total. Gas-fired, capped at **500 h/yr**, built with GE, Metlen and APC. CRU announced the measures in 2021, so it took about 2.5–3 years from decision to availability — [ESB](https://esb.ie/news---insights/ask-esb/temporary-emergency-generation). ### Inferences
- US "bridge" projects are increasingly specified as permanent prime or backup assets ("bridge-to-prime"), because rental economics over 3–5+ years favour ownership and the grid date keeps slipping.
- The Irish TEG timeline (decision 2021, plant online 2023–24) shows that even "temporary" utility-scale gas in Europe took 2.5–3 years with permitting. That is a realistic bound for a European campus bridge of ≥100 MW, unless an existing rental fleet is used.

### Gaps
- No verified European (Irish, Dutch, German, Nordic) developer-led data centre bridge project of 2024–2026 with MW and duration was found. The Aggreko Irish 18 MW case dates from about 2017–19.
- Contract durations for the US projects above are mostly undisclosed.

## 3. Cross-technology benchmark: installed cost, O&M, order-to-operation (2026)

### Takeaway
The official planning datasets (EIA AEO2026, 2025$) are well below 2025–26 market reality for gas. EIA puts frame CTs at about $1,160/kW and CCGT at about $1,030–1,090/kW. Market evidence puts recent CCGT at about $2,000–2,500/kW, new aeroderivatives at about $2,000–2,500/kW, and recip engines at about $1,700–2,000/kW all-in. SOFC is about $3,000–4,000/kW but can be on site in about 3 months. BESS turnkey prices keep falling: $117/kWh global and $177/kWh Europe (BNEF, Dec 2025). Order-to-operation times: SOFC about 3 months, mobile fleet units 1–3 months, recips/aeros 12–24 months, simple-cycle frame more than 36 months, CCGT 3–7 years.

## 4. Market-wide lead times in 2026: turbines, engines, gensets, transformers and switchgear; OEM statements

### Takeaway
Large gas turbines are effectively sold out through 2028–29: WoodMac cites six-year lead times, Siemens three or more years, and Jefferies more than 90% booked for 2028. GE Vernova's backlog plus slot reservations reached 122 GW in Q3 2026. Engines and smaller BTM turbines are less constrained but tightening (BTM about 65% booked for 2028). Every major OEM is expanding capacity: Caterpillar tripling recips by 2029–30, GE Vernova to 70–80 frames/yr, Siemens about +25% medium units by 2028, MHI doubling by 2028. Jefferies expects generation equipment to stop being the main bottleneck by about 2028–30. Transformers (about 128–144 weeks) and HV switchgear (about 44 weeks) remain binding.

## 5. Second-hand / refurbished equipment market

### Takeaway
The used aeroderivative market is "very, very hot". Used turbines in good condition trade at about $1.5–1.7 m/MW, against about $2.0–2.5 m/MW new, a discount of only about 15–30% and narrowing. Refurbished or remanufactured CF6-80C2-core packages (ProEnergy PE6000) and retired-aircraft-engine conversions (Boom) are scaling as alternatives with roughly 12-month or 2026–28 delivery.

### Cited Findings
- Used turbines in good condition cost **US$1.5–1.7 m per MW (≈€1,348–1,528/kW)**, against new at **US$2.0–2.5 m/MW (≈€1,797–2,247/kW)**, an implied discount of 15–30%. Example: two smaller used turbines totalling 29 MW sold for **US$12.5 m (~$431/kW)**, against about $35 m (~$1,207/kW) new with a 5-year wait. EthosEnergy CEO: "That secondary market is very, very hot right now", with asking prices rising (Reuters, 9 Nov 2026) — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/382890/ai-data-centers-used-jet-engines-power-turbines) [SECONDARY]
- ProEnergy PE6000 (CF6-80C2-core-based, LM6000-interchangeable, 50 MW): about 12 months from order to delivery (8-unit example), 150 units booked — [ProEnergy](https://www.proenergyservices.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Manufactured-Equipment.pdf). Crusoe ordered 13 units (650 MW) in Apr 2026 — [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/gas/data-center-power-crunch-lifts-engines-aeroderivatives-into-larger-role/)

## 6. European-specific sources (TSOs and regulators on flexible connections; European rental market)

### Takeaway
The most consequential European source found is Ireland's CRU Large Energy Users decision (CRU2025236, 12 Dec 2025). It makes on-site or proximate dispatchable generation or storage equal to 100% of de-rated MIC, participating in the SEM, a precondition for new data centre energisation, which turns "bridge" plant into permanent flexible capacity in Ireland. Aggreko's 11-country grid-code certification (Sep 2026) is the key enabler for grid-parallel rental gas in Europe. Dutch congestion is pushing even DSOs to temporary gas generation.

### Cited Findings
- **Ireland, CRU LEU decision (CRU2025236, 12 Dec 2025):** covers data centres only. Tier A is 1 to <10 MVA and Tier B is ≥10 MVA (no splitting). Both tiers require dispatchable generation or storage of **100% of MIC on a de-rated basis** (de-rating per CRM T-4 curves), not limited by run hours and participating in the SEM. Tier B may be on-site or proximate, with separate metering and connection application. A data centre "will not be permitted to become operational or ramp up to its full MIC until" that generation is delivered. Sites must also source **≥80% of annual demand from new Irish renewables** on a 6-year glide path. EirGrid and ESB Networks had to publish a connection process by 31 Mar 2026. Temporary or bridge generation is not addressed — [DLA Piper, 7 Jan 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/01/new-irish-large-energy-users-connection-policy)
- **Ireland, TEG:** 650 MW of temporary gas plant (500 h/yr cap) costs consumers €1.44 bn, extended to 2028–29 — [ESB](https://esb.ie/news---insights/ask-esb/temporary-emergency-generation); [Irish Times, Oct 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/10/09/emergency-electricity-plants-to-cost-customers-14bn/)
- **Aggreko grid-code certification** across UK, IE, DE, FR, IT, ES, NL, BE, PL, RO and CZ for the 1.5 MW fleet (Sep 2026) — [Hydrocarbon Processing](https://hydrocarbonprocessing.com/news/2026/09/aggreko-tackles-petrochemical-grid-strain-with-pan-european-grid-code-compliance-certification/); also [IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/aggreko-wins-grid-compliance-in-11-european-markets) [HEADLINE ONLY]
- **Netherlands:** Stedin's emergency gas generators around Utrecht (Jul 2026) — [NL Times](https://nltimes.nl/2026/07/21/gas-generators-placed-around-utrecht-emergency-measure-grid-congestion); a court-delayed Haarlemmermeer data centre connection (Apr 2026) [HEADLINE ONLY] — [NL Times](https://nltimes.nl/2026/04/29/judge-rules-electricity-grid-haarlemmermeer-full-data-centre-connection-delayed); "Nearly a third of Dutch grid waiting list is taken up by data centers" [HEADLINE ONLY] — [GadgetReview](https://www.gadgetreview.com/?p=420565)
- **Siemens Energy** (a European OEM) is building SGT-400 cores in Lincoln, UK, for US mobile packager Dynamis, with the first batch in 2027. This shows European-built mobile turbine capacity being committed to US data centre buyers — [Turbomachinery International](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/siemens-energy-119-sgt-400-cores-dynamis-hypermobile-power)

### Inferences
- In Ireland, any "bridge" plant for a new data centre must in practice be designed as SEM-registered, dispatchable, de-rated-capacity-compliant permanent plant. That favours gas engines or BESS sized to de-rated MIC, rather than short-term rentals.
- In grid-congested EU markets (NL, IE, parts of DE), the realistic rented option is grid-code-certified gas gensets of about 1.5 MW blocks plus BESS. Fleet depth for a 50–100 MW campus is unverified.

### Gaps
- **ENTSO-E and ACER** positions on flexible or non-firm connections for data centres, and national TSO flexible-connection products (e.g. TenneT's time-bound or non-firm contracts, National Grid ESO/NESO, Elia), were **not researched** within this budget.
- No European rental-power market report (size, fleet MW, pricing) was found.
- No German, Nordic or Spanish data centre bridge-power example was found.
