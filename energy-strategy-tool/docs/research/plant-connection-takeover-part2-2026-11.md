# Taking over a closing plant's grid connection, part 2: more countries (research, 7 November 2026)

Part B of the research run of November 2026 (OPEN-QUESTIONS 155). It extends `plant-connection-takeover-2026-11.md` (Spain, Portugal, Great Britain, Ireland, Germany) to the Netherlands, France, Italy, Belgium and the Nordic countries, and looks at Germany again. The earlier note is not changed. The question for each country: when a plant closes, does its connection pass to a new user, go to tender, or lapse? Is there any rule that gives it to a demand user?

This is a screening of public sources (regulators, operators, courts, law firms, press), not legal advice.

Confidence labels: "published and clear"; "published, but unclear which rule applies"; "worked out" (an inference, said so); "No published value found".

## In short

- **No source in any of these countries lets a demand user take over a closing plant's connection as of right.** Same answer as part 1.
- **Two things recur:**
  1. **A plant's connection is an export (feed-in) right; a data centre needs an import (offtake) right.** Where the rules say anything, they treat these as different things. Norway's best-practice guide for its queue rule says reserved capacity cannot change purpose (its example: a hydrogen project's capacity cannot be used for a data centre).
  2. **Unused or freed capacity goes back to the operator and is handed out again by the queue rules** (the Netherlands' "use it or lose it" rule, France's yearly reduction of unused connection capacity, Norway's maturity queue, Belgium's new "use it or lose it"), not passed to whoever buys the site.
- **What changes with a new owner of the same site:** in the Netherlands the transport capacity is tied to the connection, so a new user of the same site may keep it with the operator's agreement (practice, not a written rule). Norway accepts a change of owner of a queued project if its maturity information still holds. Neither covers changing a plant into a data centre.
- **Germany, revisited:** still no rule found. The new maturity process for transmission connections (2026) has no route for reusing a closed plant's connection; a reuse project would go through the ordinary process (worked out). A closing plant can also be **barred from closing** if the grid operator finds it system-relevant: it then stays in the grid reserve and frees nothing.
- **Queues are being rebuilt everywhere** around "first ready, first served" (Denmark from 1 February 2026, Germany from 2026, Norway since 2025, Finland's new principles of June 2026, and proposals in France, Belgium and Sweden). In each, a site with an existing substation may score better on maturity, but it gets no right to the old plant's capacity.

## By country

| Country | What happens to a closing plant's connection | Rule for a demand user? | Confidence | Source |

| Netherlands | No written rule on a plant's connection after closure. Transport capacity is tied to the connection, not the person; a new owner or user of the same site needs the grid operator's agreement to take over the connection and transport agreement (practice; no case law). In a congestion area, capacity left unused can be taken back: at 110 kV and above, if 50 % or less was used and 10 MW or more was left unused over 12 months (Netcode, article 7.13, ACM decision of 16 April 2024). The operator can give it to others; a new request joins the queue like any other. A data centre needs offtake capacity, a different right from a plant's feed-in. Court case: Goodman against TenneT (Rechtbank Gelderland, summary judge, 29 April 2026, ECLI:NL:RBGEL:2026:3358): TenneT need not resume a 70 MW data centre's connection at Vijfhuizen, because no connection and transport agreement had been signed and the grid is overloaded; Goodman stays on the waiting list. Separately: new hyperscale data centres (over 10 ha and at least 70 MW) are banned outside Hollands Kroon and Het Hogeland since 1 January 2024. | No. Data centres have no priority class in ACM's congestion priority framework | Published and clear (the new-user practice is a law firm's reading) | Dirkzwager, 3 Nov 2026; ACM decision ACM/UIT/612014; Rechtspraak, 29 Apr 2026; Slaughter and May (NL chapter) |
| France | No rule found on reusing a closed plant's connection. Since 1 August 2025, the connection power of sites connected for at least five years is cut each year to 125 % of their highest demand over the last five years, freeing unused capacity for the queue. RTE's "fast-track" procedure (CRE deliberation 2025-120, 7 May 2025) pre-reserves 400 kV capacity on sites chosen by the State for very large consumers (the government guide says sites above 400 MW; four of 63 identified sites have fast-track access): 30 % of connection costs on signing, a bank guarantee of 10 % of the extension works, a building permit within 16 months, and a "capacity component" charge; capacity is released if the project does not proceed. A 2026 law lets the energy minister ask RTE to reserve capacity for large industrial and digital projects. RTE's proposed queue reform ("first ready, first served", consulted until 15 October 2026) is not yet approved by CRE. | Only through the State's site selection (fast track) or a ministerial reservation; nothing tied to a closing plant | Published and clear | CRE deliberation 2025-120; French government data centre guide, 25 Nov 2025; Atlante, 26 Oct 2026 |
| Italy | No rule found. Enel switched off its last coal units at Brindisi and Civitavecchia on 31 December 2025; the government wants them kept as a cold reserve, so their capacity is not freed. A minister said in 2024 that closed plants were ideal data centre sites, because they are connected to Terna's grid; that was an idea, not a policy. Terna held 553 data centre connection requests for 101 GW at 31 August 2026. Lombardy's regional law of 3 June 2026 steers data centres to disused sites (land use, not grid access). | No | No published value found (rule); published and clear (facts) | Il Post, 10 Jan 2026; Milano Finanza, 25 Nov 2024; Key4biz, 30 Oct 2026 |
| Belgium | No rule found on closed plants. CREG's changes to the transmission connection code add binding deadlines per connection step, seriousness filters, limits on how long capacity can be reserved, and "use it or lose it" for allocated capacity left unused. Elia proposed a separate data centre category with a maximum capacity allocation (October 2025, still a proposal), and "flexible connections" that can be curtailed at peak. | No | Published and clear (no rule on closures) | A&O Shearman via LexGO, 14 Apr 2026; The Tech Capital, 27 Oct 2025 |
| Denmark | No rule found. Energinet ended "first come, first served" for large consumers from 1 February 2026: projects need documented site rights and a timeline on Energinet's template, and are grouped and prioritised by maturity, assessed in "pools". New connection agreements for very large consumers were paused during the change (still paused at the end of May 2026). | No | Published and clear (no rule on closures) | Moalem Weitemeyer, 28 Jan 2026; Data Center Knowledge, 28 May 2026 |
| Finland | No rule found. Fingrid has processed connections on maturity since its connection principles of June 2026; the government is preparing a law on priority rules when capacity runs short (proposal due autumn 2026). Fingrid has proposed that data centres flex 30 % of their demand in a fault or shortage. | No (priority rules not yet law) | Published and clear (no rule on closures) | Borenius, 26 Aug 2026 |
| Norway | Queue by maturity under the regulation on grid regulation and the energy market (NEM § 3-4; amendment of 18 December 2024). The industry's best-practice guide (June 2025) says maturity is assessed per project, and **reserved capacity cannot change purpose: a hydrogen project's capacity cannot be used for a data centre**. A change of owner is accepted if the maturity information still holds; moving a project is in principle not allowed. When a reservation is cancelled, the capacity becomes available and the grid company may offer it to the next project in the queue (worked out from the guide). | No; the guide rules out a change of purpose | Published and clear (guide, not binding) | Fornybar Norge best-practice guide, June 2025; REN on the RME proposal, 28 Sep 2023; Lovdata entry for the amendment (not readable) |
| Sweden | No rule found. Svenska kraftnät allocates on "first come, first served" for complete applications; its report of 29 April 2026 proposes capacity zones (including zones for large energy-intensive consumers) where unallocated reserved capacity can be taken back after a set period. In November 2026 it was studying a system-wide test for large connections and possibly temporary limits on new applications for very large offtakes. About 7,000 MW of data centre demand is waiting. | No (proposals only) | Published and clear (no rule on closures) | Svenska kraftnät report Svk 2025/5008, 29 Apr 2026; Energiföretagen, 4 Nov 2026 |
| Iceland | No rule found. Searched Landsnet's pages and Icelandic terms for connection terms and large users. | No published value found | No published value found | Searches of 7 Nov 2026 (no usable result) |
| Germany (revisited) | Still no rule found on whether a closed plant's connection stays available to a new user. From 2026 the four transmission operators allocate connections for consumers and storage by a maturity procedure (version 1.2, 16 June 2026; first deadline 30 June 2026): €50,000 application fee, a realisation deposit on accepting an offer (credited against the connection cost contribution), scoring on land, permits, technical concept, orders and credit. The procedure has no route for reusing a closed plant's connection; such a project would apply like any other (worked out). Ties go first to waiting time, then to demand at an existing offtake point. Changes to an existing contracted connection can bypass the procedure only with no basic change of use, no works by the operator and no bottleneck, which rules out a plant becoming a data centre (worked out). A plant must give 12 months' notice of closure (§ 13b EnWG); if the operator finds it system-relevant and the Bundesnetzagentur approves, it cannot close and stays in the grid reserve (some hard-coal plants until at least 31 March 2031). Utilities market former plant sites to data centres (RWE "energised land", Uniper, a former plant in Lübbenau for Schwarz Digits by 2027), without saying the old connection right transfers. | No | Published and clear (procedure); worked out (reuse) | Four TSOs, "Reifegradverfahren" documentation V1.2, 16 Jun 2026; Bundesnetzagentur closure page (list as of 11 Mar 2026); Reuters via cash.ch, 9 Aug 2025 |

## Proposed flag wording, one line per country

These extend the wording agreed in OPEN-QUESTIONS 155 (Spain and Portugal: tendered for renewables; Great Britain and Ireland: the campus needs its own demand connection; elsewhere "not confirmed: an assumed right").

- **Netherlands:** "Not confirmed: an assumed right. A plant's feed-in capacity is not offtake capacity; in congested areas unused capacity returns to the queue (Netcode art. 7.13), and TenneT may keep a data centre waiting until capacity exists (court ruling of 29 April 2026)."
- **France:** "Not confirmed: an assumed right. No rule found; unused connection power is cut back each year to 125 % of past demand. Fast-track capacity for large consumers is allocated by the State on chosen 400 kV sites."
- **Italy:** "Not confirmed: an assumed right. No rule found; Italy's last coal plants are being kept as a reserve."
- **Belgium:** "Not confirmed: an assumed right. No rule found; new 'use it or lose it' rules return unused capacity to the queue."
- **Denmark:** "Not confirmed: an assumed right. Large consumers are queued by maturity since 1 February 2026; no rule passes a plant's connection to a new user."
- **Finland:** "Not confirmed: an assumed right. No rule found; priority rules for scarce capacity are being prepared."
- **Norway:** "Not confirmed, and likely unavailable: reserved capacity cannot change purpose under the queue practice (NEM § 3-4 guide); a data centre needs its own maturity-assessed request."
- **Sweden:** "Not confirmed: an assumed right. No rule found; Svenska kraftnät allocates on complete applications and is reviewing large connections."
- **Iceland:** "Not confirmed: an assumed right."
- **Germany:** "Not confirmed: an assumed right. No rule found; transmission connections are allocated by a maturity procedure from 2026, and a closing plant may be held in the grid reserve."

## Questions for the owner (copied to the progress file)

1. Norway: the guide's "no change of purpose" is the clearest statement found against a takeover. Should Norway's flag say "likely unavailable" (as proposed) rather than "not confirmed"?
2. The import-against-export point applies everywhere. Should the general takeover flag say once, for every country, that a plant's export connection is not an import connection?
