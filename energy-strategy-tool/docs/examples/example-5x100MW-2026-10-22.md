# Worked example: 5 × 100 MW campus, late and undersized grid (2026-10-22)

Asked for by the owner on 2026-10-22 to show how the agreed "power adequacy first" rule (OPEN-QUESTIONS 38) would pick the best three options. Run with the engine on branch `feature/defaults-research` (commit after b7429be, which includes the batch 1 defaults approved on 2026-10-22: EU ETS €88.30/t, UK ETS €75/t). Everything else is at its default, labelled indicative — unverified. Profiles are synthetic. The adequacy-first ranking is not built yet: the run was ranked by today's engine and then re-ranked by hand with the rule below.

## Inputs

| Input | Value |

| Analysis start | January 2027 |
| Buildings (phases) | 5 × 100 MW IT, energised January 2029, 2030, 2031, 2032 and 2033 |

| Run | 2,000 options screened (sampled from 42,771 combinations), full 8,760-hour model, 20 s |

## Shortfall

- **Before the grid** (2029–2030): everything comes from on site; the largest shortfall is 219 MW (December 2030, building 1 at full load and building 2 ramping).
- **After the grid** (2031 onwards): the grid alone supports **425 MW of IT** (480.5 MW ÷ 1.13). At the full contracted 500 MW the permanent shortfall is **85 MW**. At the 80 % average utilisation there is none, which is why the dispatch shows no unmet energy for most options.

## Adequacy test used

An option is adequate when it has no unmet energy in any hour **and** no power gap against the design load (100 % of contracted IT) in any month. Tested on dispatch alone (80 % utilisation), 1,741 of 2,000 options pass. Tested against the design load, 1,512 pass. The 229 options in between serve the average load but are 85 MW short of the contracted load. For a customer who can draw 100 % of their IT at any time, they are not adequate. (Whether the test is at full contracted IT is to be confirmed with the owner.)

## What the example shows about the tool

1. Adequacy has to be tested at the full contracted IT load; at average utilisation the 85 MW permanent shortfall is invisible.
2. The top of the list repeats one strategy (the owner's page 66 note; OPEN-QUESTIONS 24).
3. Strategies 1 and 3 lose about four points on time-to-power. The cause is that "full load" is the month supply first reaches the full 565 MW, not the month it meets the demand of the buildings energised so far (related to KNOWN-ISSUES item 9).
4. Firm prime plant (C4, for example CHP) is sized to the average load (450 MW), not to the 85 MW shortfall: proposal part 3 (package 3b).
5. Availability (nines) is not calculated: resilience is a pass/fail check. Ranking for five nines needs an availability measure.
